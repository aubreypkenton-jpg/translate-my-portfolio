Methodology

How every number is calculated

Every figure on the report comes from a formula on this page applied to daily closing prices from Yahoo Finance. Every sentence is a fixed template with those numbers plugged in. There is no AI model writing text or estimating anything.

Educational only. This tool is not investment, tax, or legal advice and is not a substitute for a licensed professional. Historical results do not predict future returns.

Data source

Daily closing prices (adjusted for splits and dividends) for up to {{ config.HISTORY_PERIOD|replace('y', ' years') }}, plus sector, fund type, expense ratio, and dividend yield, come from Yahoo Finance through the free, open-source yfinance library. No API key or paid feed is used. The S&P 500 benchmark is the SPDR S&P 500 ETF ({{ config.BENCHMARK_TICKER }}), a fund that tracks the index. Prices are cached for {{ (config.CACHE_TTL_SECONDS // 60) }} minutes. Yahoo's data is unofficial and can contain gaps or errors; it is appropriate for education, not for trading decisions.

Weights and portfolio history

weight_i = (shares_i × latest price_i) / total portfolio value

Portfolio history uses only the dates on which every holding has a price, so a recently listed stock shortens the window for the whole portfolio (the report states the exact dates). Daily portfolio return is the weighted sum of each holding's daily return using today's weights, which is equivalent to rebalancing back to today's mix every day. Real portfolios drift between rebalances, so treat this as "how this mix has behaved," not "what you actually earned."

r_portfolio,t = Σ weight_i × r_i,t value index starts at 1.0 and compounds: V_t = V_(t-1) × (1 + r_portfolio,t)

Concentration and the Herfindahl-Hirschman index

HHI = Σ weight_i² effective number of holdings = 1 / HHI

The HHI is the same index regulators use to measure market concentration. Ten equal holdings give HHI 0.10 (10 effective holdings); one holding at 91% gives about 1.2. Flags: any single holding above {{ config.CONCENTRATION_FLAG_PCT|int }}%, or any single sector above {{ config.SECTOR_FLAG_PCT|int }}% (diversified funds are not counted as a "sector"). Asset class (individual stock, ETF, mutual fund) comes from Yahoo's quote type.

Compound annual growth rate (CAGR)

CAGR = (ending value / starting value)^(1 / years) − 1 years = trading days / 252

Volatility

annualized volatility = standard deviation(daily returns) × √252

252 is the approximate number of US trading days in a year. Per-holding volatility uses each holding's own full history; portfolio volatility uses the shared window above.

Maximum drawdown

drawdown_t = V_t / max(V_0 … V_t) − 1 max drawdown = min over t of drawdown_t

The largest peak-to-trough loss you would have had to sit through.

Sharpe and Sortino ratios

daily risk-free rate = (1 + annual rate)^(1/252) − 1 Sharpe = mean(r − r_f) / stdev(r) × √252 Sortino = mean(r − r_f) / √mean(min(r − r_f, 0)²) × √252

Both use daily returns over the shared window. The risk-free rate defaults to {{ '%.2f'|format(config.RISK_FREE_RATE * 100) }}% and can be changed on the main page; the current 3-month Treasury bill yield is the usual choice. Sortino only counts downside deviation as risk. Rule of thumb: Sharpe above 1 is strong; below 0 means the portfolio didn't beat cash after accounting for risk over that period.

Beta and the S&P 500 benchmark

beta = covariance(r_portfolio, r_SPY) / variance(r_SPY)

Every "vs. the S&P 500" figure (CAGR, volatility, drawdown, Sharpe, beta) is computed on exactly the same dates as the portfolio, so the comparison is like for like.

Correlation

Pearson correlation of daily returns for each pair of holdings, from −1 (move opposite) to +1 (move together). Pairs above {{ config.HIGH_CORRELATION }} are flagged. A correlation of 0.85 does not mean the two "move together 85% of the time"; it measures how tightly their daily moves line up.

Crash scenarios

For each window, each held ticker's real price change from the first to the last trading day is weighted by today's weights. If a holding didn't exist yet, it's excluded, the result is re-weighted over the holdings that did, and the coverage percentage is shown. The window dates are the S&P 500's peak and trough, and SPY's own change over the same dates is shown alongside.

Fund fees and dividend yield

value-weighted expense ratio = Σ (value_i × expense ratio_i) / Σ value_i (funds with a reported ratio only) estimated annual fees = Σ value_i × expense ratio_i

Funds charging more than {{ '%.2f'|format(config.HIGH_EXPENSE_RATIO_PCT) }}% a year and yields above {{ config.HIGH_DIVIDEND_YIELD_PCT|int }}% are flagged for a second look. Both figures come from Yahoo Finance and can lag the fund's latest prospectus.

Capital gains (US federal)

A position is long-term if held more than one year. The holding period starts the day after purchase, so shares bought January 10, 2023 become long-term on January 11, 2024. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income; long-term gains get lower rates (0%, 15% or 20% federally, depending on income). Unrealized gain = (current price − price paid) × shares. If several lots of the same ticker are entered, they're combined at a weighted-average cost, and the most recent purchase date is used (the conservative choice). Losses can offset gains, subject to the wash-sale rule (no buying a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale). Sources: IRS Publication 550, IRS Topic 409. This is not tax advice; state taxes, the net investment income tax, and your situation change the answer.

Behavioral flags

  • Recency bias: purchase price more than {{ config.RECENCY_BIAS_RUNUP_PCT|int }}% above the close 30 trading days earlier.
  • Loss aversion: down more than {{ config.LOSS_AVERSION_LOSS_PCT|abs|int }}% and held over {{ config.LOSS_AVERSION_MIN_HOLD_DAYS }} days.
  • Fast, big bet: more than {{ config.CONCENTRATED_RECENT_BUY_PCT|int }}% of the portfolio, bought within {{ config.CONCENTRATED_RECENT_BUY_DAYS }} days.

These thresholds are simple, transparent defaults, not calibrated against the behavioral-finance literature.

Rebalance comparison

The target portfolio keeps the same total dollars at your target weights and is analyzed with exactly the same code as your real one. Trades = target value − current value per ticker. Sales of short-term gains are flagged. Commissions, spreads, and taxes are ignored. It shows how the target mix would have behaved, not how it will.

Risk score (0 to 100)

The average of five sub-scores, each scaled to 0 to 100 against a fixed cap: largest holding ({{ config.RISK_SCORE_CAPS.concentration|int }}% = 100), largest sector ({{ config.RISK_SCORE_CAPS.sector|int }}%), volatility ({{ config.RISK_SCORE_CAPS.volatility|int }}%), highest pairwise correlation (1.0), and max drawdown ({{ config.RISK_SCORE_CAPS.drawdown|int }}%). Bands: under 25 Low, under 50 Moderate, under 75 High, otherwise Very High. It's a heuristic for tracking change over time, not a validated risk model.

Limitations

  • Past performance does not predict future results; five years is a short sample that includes one bull market and two sharp drawdowns.
  • Yahoo Finance data is unofficial and occasionally missing or wrong for thinly traded or non-US symbols.
  • Constant-weight history ignores drift, cash flows, and the timing of your actual purchases.
  • Sector labels for funds are "diversified"; the tool doesn't look through to each fund's holdings, so two funds that own the same stocks can hide overlap (correlation partly catches it).
  • Only the holdings you type in are known. There's no brokerage connection, and nothing is stored.
