About & methodology

What this is

You type in what you own — ticker, shares, and optionally what you paid and when. The app fetches real price history for each holding and explains, in plain English, how risky and diversified it actually is, based on what genuinely happened to those prices, not a generic lesson.

Why it's trustworthy

Every sentence you see is built from a number computed from real fetched price data. There's no AI model writing the explanations — they're template sentences with real numbers plugged in. Nothing is invented, estimated by a language model, or guessed.

This app doesn't make money whether you buy, sell, or do nothing — there's no brokerage relationship, no affiliate links, no incentive to nudge you toward activity.

How each number is calculated

Concentration is simply each holding's value divided by total portfolio value. A single holding above 25% of the portfolio, or a single sector above 50%, is flagged.

Volatility is the annualized standard deviation of daily returns over the available price history (up to 5 years).

Max drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline in the available price history for each holding, and for the portfolio as a whole (weighted by your position sizes).

Correlation measures how closely two holdings' daily price moves track each other, from -1 (perfectly opposite) to 1 (perfectly together). Above 0.7 is flagged as meaningfully overlapping.

Historical crash scenarios apply the real price change each currently-held ticker experienced during the 2020 COVID crash (Feb 19 – Mar 23, 2020) and the 2022 bear market (Jan 3 – Oct 12, 2022), weighted by today's position sizes. If a ticker didn't exist yet during a window, it's excluded and the coverage percentage says so.

Sharpe ratio is (annualized return − a 4% assumed risk-free rate) divided by annualized volatility, computed over the overlapping history of your holdings.

Risk score (0-100) is a simple equal-weighted blend of concentration, sector concentration, volatility, correlation, and max drawdown, each scaled against a fixed cap. It's a heuristic meant to give you one number to watch move over time — not a calibrated institutional risk model.

Behavioral flags are pattern-matches against your entered purchase price and date: buying more than 15% above the price from 30 trading days earlier (recency bias), holding a position down more than 20% for over 180 days (loss aversion), or putting more than 20% of the portfolio into something bought within the last 30 days (a fast, large bet).

Tax notes are based on IRS short-term vs. long-term capital gains treatment: under a year held is short-term (taxed as ordinary income), a year or more is long-term (lower rate). This is general information, not tax advice — consult a real tax professional for your specific situation.

Data source

Price and sector data comes from Yahoo Finance via the free yfinance library. No API key, no paid data feed — appropriate for personal use, not institutional-scale accuracy guarantees.

Limitations

This is a personal tool, not investment advice, and not a substitute for a financial advisor. The risk score and thresholds are simple heuristics, not validated against formal risk models. Manual entry means it only knows what you tell it — it doesn't connect to your actual brokerage account.
